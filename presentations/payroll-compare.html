TL;DR

The one-paragraph read before the tables.

Both we and Cloak are Groth16 shielded pools on Solana hiding sender/recipient/amount links. Cloak is a general-purpose private finance layer, live on mainnet beta with viewing-key compliance. We are a payroll-specialized system with measured, logged end-to-end benchmarks (210 salaries in 67.6s standard circuit; 11.32s batched bench) that Cloak does not publish. Our main gaps: no viewing keys or audit tooling, SOL-only today, and no mainnet audit or volume track record.

Where we trail

Architecture deep dive: Cloak vs ours

Same proof system, different pool model, different trust and sync story. Cloak figures are claimed on their site; ours are measured, logged runs.

Dimension Cloak Ours (Charon)
Pool model UTXO shielded pool: notes you hold, the backup file is the money. Merkle-tree commitment pool with spend nullifiers; a note is a 169-byte encrypted v2 note.
Proof system Groth16, generated client-side in browser. Their site claims "under 3 seconds" proof and a 4.4s total pipeline breakdown (witness 840ms, Groth16 prover 1.4s, relay submission 320ms, chain confirmation 1.8s); on-chain verify < 50ms claimed on their site. Groth16 (bn254). Two measured paths: browser snarkjs ~4s/proof, and relayer-side Rust arkworks prover at 0.28s/proof in-process measured (bench at payroll/rust-prover-bench).
Sync / scanning User scans the chain with viewing key nk; recoverable deposit UTXO rebuilds notes from chain. Relayer keeps a D1 (Cloudflare SQL) mirror of tree plus note index with lazy sync; employees scan via the relayer API so a phone needs no full scan.
Gas model Relayer submits; wallet pays no gas and never appears as sender; shield is free. Same shape: relayer broadcasts; employee claims are fully gasless (employees need zero SOL); an employer run needs one signature (zero once the vault is funded).
Fees 0.3% on exit plus a small fixed fee (0.005 SOL or 0.45 USDC/USDT) claimed. Relayer fee in basis points with a configurable minimum, enforced on-chain (ext > fee check).
Batch payroll "Payroll (batch private payouts in USDC or USDT) ... build on the same rails" - no public benchmark. Purpose-built batch circuit: 4 salaries per proof (batch5, discriminator 8); SPL-memo pad packs 8 salaries per tx. Measured 210 employees in 12.7s (bench) and 11.32s full-pipeline bench; append_many instruction at 195.5k CU per transact measured.
Compliance Viewing keys (disclose own history only, cannot move funds) plus sanctions screening before broadcast. not shipped Deposits and exits are public by design; employer keeps per-run receipts; recipient-scoped viewing keys on roadmap.
Tokens SOL, USDC, USDT plus Jupiter swaps plus EVM bridge-in. SOL today; the v2 note format is token-agnostic, SPL stables are next.
Ecosystem proof Mainnet beta, audited program, volume marks claimed. Local-validator benchmarks, full E2E tests, open program tests; no mainnet deployment yet.

Two honest lists

No spin: what we have that they do not, and what they have that we do not.

Where we are aheadmeasured
  • Measured and logged end-to-end payroll runs - their payroll has no public numbers.
  • Batch payout circuit + memo packing - 8 salaries per tx.
  • Employer console UX - CSV upload, live run progress, receipts, one-click resume of failed rows.
  • Gasless employee claims with zero-SOL wallets.
  • Recipients registry - notes encrypted to published recipient keys.
  • Serverless relayer on Cloudflare Workers + D1.
Where they are aheadour gaps
  • Live on mainnet with an independent audit.
  • Viewing keys + sanctions screening.
  • Stablecoin-first (USDC/USDT) + swaps + cross-chain bridge-in.
  • Payment links and .sol domain support.
  • Public volume / track record.
